# Annual Report of the Lunar Cheese Mining Consortium (LCMC), Fiscal Year 2051

*Prepared by the Office of Dairy Selenology, Mare Lactis Field Station.
Distribution: shareholders, the Board of Rinds, and any mouse who can read.*

## 1. Executive summary

The Consortium completed its tenth year of continuous operations on the lunar
surface. Extraction from the three active craters exceeded forecasts by 7 %,
largely because the Camembert Ridge deposit turned out to be *considerably*
riper than the survey drones reported. Settlement populations kept growing,
the feral cheese-mouse population did too, and the maturation burn of the
Great Wheel of Emmental (Section 4) went almost entirely to plan.

All tables below follow the Consortium's reporting convention: the caption
sits **above** the table it describes.

## 3. Settlements

The three mining settlements grew steadily. Crater Gouda remains the largest,
while Camembert Ridge, founded only in 2044, is catching up fast. Population
counts are taken every January by the census robot BEAN-COUNTER; the number of
feral cheese mice is an estimate, because the mice refuse to fill in the forms.

## 4. The Great Wheel of Emmental

Every year the Consortium matures one ceremonial Great Wheel of Emmental
(diameter 4.2 m) in the vacuum-insulated cave at Brie Basin. The 14-day
maturation burn is monitored by thermocouples embedded at the core and just
below the rind. Hole formation is counted by ultrasonic tomography; the aroma
index is measured by a panel of three volunteer noses.

## 5. Outlook

The Board of Rinds has approved a fourth settlement at Roquefort Rille for
FY2053, subject to a satisfactory mouse-containment plan. Shareholders are
reminded that cheese extracted on the Moon may not be eaten on the Moon before
the end of the quarantine period.
